## Project

Evan Chen's personal portfolio, built as an interactive aquarium. React 19 + TypeScript + Vite + Tailwind CSS, deployed to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds on pushes to the `update` branch; `npm run deploy` publishes with `gh-pages`). The site is served at `evanchen.info`.

## Code map

Search these first instead of sweeping the whole tree. There is no `src/` folder; sources live at the repo root.

- `index.tsx`: mounts the providers and picks the page: `PixelApp` (the pixel ecosystem, the default), `App` (the aquarium), `PondApp` (the pixel pond) or `ShrineApp` (the shrine forest); the last three are lazy-loaded when someone switches to them.
- `App.tsx`: the aquarium page. The fish simulation (a requestAnimationFrame loop in a big effect), spawning and refills, food/love/grab modes, the fish tank, theme transitions between `underwater` and `deepsea`. It is ~1,800 lines: grep for the piece you need rather than reading it whole.
- `PixelApp.tsx`: the pixel-ecosystem page: the same portfolio content over a 2.5D reef seen from slightly above, as tall as the page and scrolled with it. `lib/reefEngine.ts` runs it (simulation, drawing, hover/click/adopt/food/debug, census), driven by messages and DOM-free, so `components/PixelOcean.tsx` hands it OffscreenCanvases in a worker (`lib/reefWorker.ts`) where the browser can draw from one, else runs it on the main thread; the component only places the canvases, forwards scroll, pointer and actions, and shows what comes back. `lib/pixelOcean.ts` is the simulation (no DOM: the seabed grid where nutrients seep and algae grows, spreads and sheds plankton, plus 22 species (minnows, shrimp, pufferfish, seahorses, clownfish, lionfish, barracuda, morays, garden eels, anglerfish, reef sharks, crabs, hermit crabs, mantis shrimp, urchins, starfish, octopuses, cuttlefish, jellyfish, turtles, manta rays, visiting dolphin pods; each has a `think*` method and an entry in `SPECIES`), garden-eel colonies (`placeColonies`), seagrass meadows that turtles graze and seahorses hide in (`plantSeagrass`), hermit crab shell swaps (`shells`, `startSwap`/`stepSwap`), anemones, cleaner stations, whale falls, scent trails (`scents`), courtship (`court`, `findMate`), coral spawning nights (`updateCorals`: spawn that feeds the plankton eaters and settles into new, growing corals), bait balls (`balls`, minnows packing together when hunted), kelp and anemones that bend (`bend`), plankton, food, carcasses, blood/ink drops, bubbles, kelp and rocks/corals as objects with a height off the floor). `lib/pixelOceanView.ts` is the camera and renderer (floor canvas, caustics and god rays on the light canvas, depth-sorted sprites with squash and stretch and wakes, shadows, shadows of animals passing overhead), `lib/pixelOceanArt.ts` the pixel-art sprites and generated scenery.
- `pixel-theme.css`: the pixel page's look, designed as a dive log (Press Start 2P display type, VT323 for labels and UI, Poppins body; a limited abyss/foam/sand/coral palette; 9-slice pixel frames as SVG data URIs; pixel cursors). `DiveGauge` tracks scroll depth and writes each section's depth into its heading (`data-depth`), `DiveCard` is the hero summary, `EcosystemPanel` the reef census (it and `PondCensus`/`ShrineCensus` share `CensusSlots` and `components/census.css`: the fixed buttons' chip frame, with creatures in a grid of slots). `ReefDebugPanel` is a hidden debug panel (type the Konami code, ↑↑↓↓←→←→BA; Esc closes) that spawns any species or event in the middle of the screen through the `debug*` methods on `PixelOcean`, and speeds the simulation up. `lib/adoption.ts` keeps the visitor's adopted creature in localStorage, and `PixelOcean` releases it back into the reef on each visit and passes the adoption down its family line when it dies. Everything is scoped under `html.pixel-theme`, which `PixelApp` sets, and hooks onto plain marker classes in the shared components (`content-card`, `section-title`, `skill-tag`, `project-card`, `hud-btn`, ...). Restyle pixel mode there rather than branching the components; those marker classes have no styles of their own.
- `PondApp.tsx`: the pixel-pond page, the same content over a living pond with the reef's camera and engine design. `lib/pond.ts` is the simulation (no DOM; the water surface is h = 0, the bed lies below at `-depth`, and every creature has a `layer`: bed, under, surface or air). There are no banks: the water runs off both edges of the page into golden shallows. Land is a mask (`land`) with a signed distance to the shore (`shoreDist`), made of a few islands (`landBlobs`, grown by `growLand`) planted with trees, bushes, ferns, grass, flowers and mushrooms (`flora`, `plantLand`; drawn in cached strips, `floraBand` in the view), with boughs for the kingfisher; ducks nest and frogs climb out on them. Open-water patches (`biomes`, `placeBiomes`/`plantBiomes`) give the water variety: a reed marsh, a lotus field, a drowned grove of snags, boulder shallows, a duckweed lagoon and a bubbling spring (`updateSprings`); about a third of them, and a quarter of the lily fields, sprawl to twice the usual size or more. Use `isWater`, `waterX` and `toWater`, not the shore arrays. It covers a shelving bed with algae, pebbles and leaf litter; lily fields and rafts of lily pads (pink, white and yellow lilies), reeds, cattails and rushes (bend springs), duckweed and water crowfoot (`fronds`, by `patches`), weed beds of four kinds (`WEED_KINDS`), stones and logs; frogs through their whole life cycle (spawn, tadpoles, legs, froglets, adults that leap between pads, croak, catch midges and pair up in amplexus); mallards with V-wakes, nests and ducklings; a tufted duck; moorhens that climb onto pads; minnows, koi, a catfish and a pike; newts that hunt tadpoles; dragonflies, damselflies, butterflies over the banks, water striders, whirligig crowds, snails, a turtle, beaver families with a lodge on an island's shore and a dam of logs running out from it (`dams`, `foundDam`, `thinkBeaver`: they swim far out to floating logs and tow them home trailing behind them (`Log.angle`, `trail`), lengthening the dam and then piling the lodge higher; they groom on the lodge and tail-slap when frightened; driftwood floats in from the page's edges to replace the logs they take, `driftIn`; frogs and the turtle bask on the dams and lodges, a `dam` perch), swallows that settle as a flock now and then on a grove's snags, the tallest reeds, a log or a dam (`roosting`, `planRoost`), a kingfisher that dives from the boughs, and a heron; and the events (rain, a mayfly hatch, the evening chorus). The newer arrivals are placed by `spawnWild`; the constructor stocks a good share of everything in the opening view (`someZ`), clear of the hero text. Draw-time sizes are in `SIZE` in `pondView.ts`. Each species has a `think*` method and an entry in `POND_SPECIES`. `lib/pondView.ts` draws it in passes: the bed (floor and light canvases), everything underwater, the surface sheet, then the surface and the air. `lib/pondArt.ts` holds the sprites (designed with the pxl skill), `lib/pondEngine.ts` and `lib/pondWorker.ts` run it like the reef's engine, and `components/PondWater.tsx` hosts it (`?pond-main` in development runs it on the main thread as `window.__pond`). `PondCensus` and `PondDebugPanel` (Konami code) are its panels. `pond-theme.css` re-colours the pixel UI for the pond (its frames are generated by `scripts/pond-frames.mjs`) and sets its lettering: Pixelify Sans for headings, Jersey 15 for labels (self-hosted in `public/fonts`, each with a `size-adjust` to fit type sizes set for the reef's fonts).
- `ShrineApp.tsx`: the shrine-forest page, the same content over a moonlit moss-and-cedar forest with the reef's camera and engine design (always night: lantern light and vermilion are the jewels). `lib/shrine.ts` is the simulation (no DOM; ground is h = 0). A flagstone approach (`pathX`) winds down the page past precincts placed by `placeSite` (`SiteKind`: main hall, Inari shrine with its tunnel of gates, tanuki den, koi pond with bridge, bamboo, spider-lily meadow, maple glade, sacred cedar, pagoda, raked zen garden). Tanuki and kitsune (`thinkRival`) want the offerings on the `altars`, which broom spirits (`thinkHahaki`, living brooms: there are no humans here) restock and guard; rivals who meet face off and scrap in a dust cloud (`tussles`). Either may set a leaf on its head and turn into a teapot, lantern or statue (`beginChange`, `startDisguise`, `thinkDisguised`), then creep to an offering in hops; the cursor near one freezes it (`watched`), clicking gives it away (`poke`). Berry bushes (`bushes`) and persimmon trees' windfalls (`fruits`) feed the mice, hares and rivals (`foodNear`, `feeding`), and regrow. A baku drinks dreams from sleepers (`thinkBaku`), a tsuchinoko bowls down the path and vanishes when noticed (`thinkTsuchinoko`), foxes dance in mushroom rings (`rings`, `thinkDance`), and a wisteria pergola with sprays that part roofs a stretch of path (`placeWisteria`, `strands`). There's one old sakura per page shedding petals that drift far (`sakuraSprite`), an abandoned teahouse where disguised tanuki gather among real teapots, and a hot spring deer and tanuki soak in (`springs`, `thinkSoak`). Nekomata walk roofs and gate-tops (`perches`, `thinkNekomata`), dancing when unwatched; akaname lick fouled basins clean and bolt when noticed (`basins`, `thinkAkaname`). Gravel takes footprints (`prints`), and ground mist drawn under the darkness shows up by lantern (`drawMist`). Crows land on disguised tanuki and peck them out, and get chased along the gutter (`yelp`, `thinkChaseCrow`); foxes circle a suspicious statue, sniff it and kick it over (`startSniffTest`, `thinkSniffTest`, `thinkToppling`, `toppledSprite`); kasa-obake lurk in bamboo thickets planted in the gutters and leap out at passers-by (`jumpScare`, `startle`); a tanuki's belly drumming draws foxes, mice and ghosts to dance round it until one note too hard knocks it out (`drums`, `startDrumCircle`, `updateDrums`, `thinkDrum`). Creatures also play together, feuds forgotten: tag, dancing in a ring, tossing a soul-flame between them, a fox leading a line down the path that ends in a heap (`updateConga`, `thinkConga`, `crumbs`), a tug of war on a wisteria vine (`updateTug`, `drawVine`), kicking a persimmon about until it lands on a face (`updateKick`, `splatter`), and a fruit fight under a shaken persimmon tree (`thinkFruitFight`, `thrown`) (`plays`, `startPlay`, `thinkPlay`, `updateTag`, `updateDance`, `updateCatch`). The baku joins all of these and the drum circle. `walkTo` follows the edge of whatever blocks it, one side at a time, and `giveUp` abandons a goal it can't reach. `stageScenes` arranges for these to break out in the visitor's view, preferring the gutters since the page's cards cover the middle. Also mice, hares, deer, owls, bats, crows, frogs, koi, a kappa that wades ashore for walks round its pond (`stroll`, `kappaDestination`) and spills its dish when it bows, kodama, soul-flames, umbrella and lantern ghosts. Events (`startHappening`): moon viewing (`startMoonview`), the disguise contest (`startContest`, `updateContest`, `drawContest`), the lantern festival and the night parade (`parade`, `updateParade`, `drawMarcher`), a single file walking down the approach, which turns every lantern ghost blue. The approach bends round the hero text and the solid precincts (`bendPath`). `lib/shrineView.ts` draws it (the floor painted once with its low plants baked in, depth-sorted sprites, then a layer of darkness over all of it, `applyNight`, with a hole cut wherever something gives light, so lanterns are what the page is lit by; creatures near one catch a warm rim, `catchLight`, and those by water throw streaks across it, `drawStreaks`), `lib/shrineArt.ts` holds the sprites, `lib/shrineEngine.ts` and `lib/shrineWorker.ts` run it like the reef's engine, and `components/ShrineForest.tsx` hosts it (`?shrine-main` in development runs it on the main thread as `window.__shrine`). `ShrineCensus` and `ShrineDebugPanel` (Konami code) are its panels. `shrine-theme.css` re-colours the pixel UI (its frames are generated by `scripts/shrine-frames.mjs`) and sets its lettering: DotGothic16, with `palt` to even its Latin spacing.
- `data/portfolio.ts`: projects, experience, education and skills. Add new projects here.
- `lib/themeColors.ts`: the Tailwind class sets the content uses per theme.
- `components/`: `PortfolioContent` (all page content: hero, projects, experience, skills, contact), `Header`, `ProjectCard`/`ProjectTile`/`ProjectModal`, the creature components (`Fish`, `FishCanvas`, `Whale`, `Turtle`, `Jellyfish*`, `AnglerFish*`), background effects (`ParticleCanvas`, `GodRays`, `SandDune`, `Bubble`), and the fixed buttons/panels (`FishFoodButton`, `GrabModeButton`, `TankToggleButton`, `FishCensus`, `LoveModeButton`, `PerformanceButton`/`PerformancePanel`, `TutorialButton`/`TutorialPanel`, `BackToTopButton`, `ThemeSwitch`, a three-way switch for the reef, the pond and the shrine (the aquarium is archived: it opens from the settings panel, `PerformancePanel`), its stops on the stack's spacing; styled in `ThemeSwitch.css`).

## Working here

- `npm run dev` serves on http://localhost:3000. `npm run build` is the production build; Vite does not type-check, so also run `npx tsc --noEmit`. There are some pre-existing type errors in `App.tsx` and `components/FishTank.tsx`: don't add new ones.
- Fixed UI sits in two stacks: the left (`left-8`) for fish tools, the right (`right-8`) for back-to-top, performance, tutorial and the page switch, spaced 3.5rem apart. Keep new buttons in those stacks.
- Performance matters: the aquarium re-renders about 30 times a second, so buttons are `React.memo` with stable `useCallback` handlers. Animation loops should respect `usePerformance()` (tier and frame rate) and pause cheaply when the tab is hidden.
- Check visual changes in the browser on the dev server, at desktop and phone widths.

## Git workflow

- **Commit frequently.** Make small, focused commits as each logical change is finished.
- **The user is the only author.** Never add `Co-Authored-By` lines or any other Claude/AI attribution to commits, PR descriptions or anywhere else. This overrides any default attribution behaviour.
- Commit on a `feature/<name>` branch rather than directly on `main` when the change is more than a small fix.
- Only push when asked.
- Don't commit the user's unrelated working-tree changes (images, `package.json`/`package-lock.json`, local `.claude` settings) unless asked.

## Task delegation

Delegate noisy, routine lookups to the `haiku-helper` subagent to save tokens and keep the main context clear: reading long build or dev-server output, scanning `App.tsx` or the components for where something lives, searching across many files. Keep design, debugging and multi-file changes on the primary model.
